Supplementary material
Who Gets an ESG Score? Coverage Selection and Firm Value in Five Southeast Asian Markets
This supplementary material accompanies "Who Gets an ESG Score? Coverage Selection and Firm Value in Five Southeast Asian Markets." It presents the supplementary tables and figures first and then Appendix A, which discusses additional results. The pre-analysis plan is provided with the replication package.

Appendix A. Additional robustness results
A.1. Event Year 4 (Section 4.1)
Only 141 scored firms from 6 cohorts reach event year 4; therefore, we plot that year in Figure 1 but exclude it from the headline estimate. Its estimates are −0.077 for MTB (p = 0.209) and 0.088 for market capitalization (p = 0.153).
A.2. Further Timing Variants of the One-Year-Later Dating (Section 4.2)
Under the one-year-later dating, the decline in log MTB loses significance once M̄ in the relative-magnitude bound reaches 0.23. Holding total assets at the original base year gives −0.074 (p = 0.002), so the covariate timing does not drive the one-year-later estimate. Dating coverage two years later gives −0.048 (95% interval −0.099 to 0.000), a smaller decline that is no longer significant at the 5% level.
A.3. Reconciling the One-Year-Later and Baseline Event-Time Paths (Section 4.2, Figure S2)
The baseline path in Figure 1 does not show the one-year-later decline because its later event years come from early cohorts, while its first post-coverage years pool all cohorts. Re-based within each cohort to the first recorded year, the baseline point estimates for event years 3 and 4 are −0.083 and −0.167, respectively. These lie closer to the corresponding one-year-later estimates of −0.138 and −0.200 than the pooled baseline path suggests. We report the re-based values without standard errors, as a reconciliation of the two paths rather than a test. Table 4 of the paper carries out the full step-by-step reconciliation on a common sample.
A.4. Size Covariate and Support (Section 4.3, Table S4, Figure S2)
Table S4 and Figure S2 vary the size covariate and support; Table 5 of the paper reports the full set of variants. Scored-firm counts differ slightly across tables for the same estimate: Tables 4, 5, and 7 count firms in the averaged event years, while Tables 2, 3, S9, and S11 count firms in any event-time cell. The run-up is smaller without conditioning on base-year total assets. Without covariates, the MTB run-up from event year −5 to the base year is 6.6%, weaker than the baseline (event year −5 p = 0.068; pre-trend p = 0.028), and the market capitalization run-up is 24.0%. With total assets measured two years before the base year, it is 8.0% for MTB (pre-trend p = 0.176) and 20.3% for market capitalization.
A.5. Decomposition of the TWFE Gap (Section 4.3, Table S5)
Table S5 shows the decomposition of the gap between the static TWFE estimate and the headline MTB estimate. Restricting the regression to never-scored controls and event years −5 to 3 changes the estimate to only 0.058, while the heterogeneity-robust estimator gives 0.025 without covariates and 0.002 with them. Comparing each cohort with its own base year, rather than pooling all years, accounts for approximately half of the gap. Conditioning on base-year total assets accounts for most of the rest, and the choice of controls and window for little.
A.6. Market-by-Market and Early Versus Late Cohorts (Section 4.4, Table S11)
Estimated market by market in Table S11, the MTB effect is 0.009 in Malaysia, with 372 scored firms, and 0.027 in Thailand, with 157. Indonesia, the Philippines, and Singapore have few scored firms and imprecise estimates. The 58 firms first scored by 2019 that enter the MTB estimate show a positive post-coverage MTB estimate of 0.094 (p = 0.047; 95% interval 0.002 to 0.185), and the larger group first scored from 2020 shows −0.010 (p = 0.687). The early estimate rests on few firms, is one of many exploratory comparisons, and is not adjusted for multiple testing, so we do not read it as evidence of a gain for early entrants.
A.7. Breadth of the Malaysian Wave (Section 4.1, Figure 2)
The Malaysian wave added 289 firms in two years, yet 565 Malaysian firms remained never-scored, so it was broad but not market-wide.
A.8. Composition of the Pooled Estimate (Section 4.4, Table S10)
Table S10 counts scored firm-cells, one for each scored firm in each post-coverage cell of the ln(MTB) estimate, by market and by coverage wave. These counts are the weights of the headline estimate.
A.9. Dating by Wave (Section 4.2, Table S6)
Table S6 reports, for each wave, the average change with coverage dated at the first recorded score year and one year later, estimated against all never-scored firms.
A.10. Firm-years with and Without an MTB Ratio (Section 4.3, Table S8)
Table S8 compares scored and never-scored firm-years with and without an MTB ratio in the estimation sample. Book leverage above 0.5 and Indonesian listing are more common among firm-years without an MTB ratio.
A.11. Other Robustness Checks (Section 4.3)
The four pre-specified robustness checks in Table 3 give MTB effects of −0.001 with not-yet-scored firms among the controls, 0.025 without covariates, 0.031 without the 2020 and 2021 cohorts, and 0.016 when control firms below the size range of scored firms are dropped. Under the last restriction the market capitalization effect shrinks to −0.007 and the total assets effect is 0.101, against 0.011 without covariates. Removing a linear pre-trend that passes through zero in the base year gives a negative MTB effect because a continued run-up would have raised valuations further. Adding market capitalization growth from g − 3 to the base year to the covariates does not settle the reversal question, because control firms with similar past growth can themselves revert. The placebo gives 0.134 for log market capitalization (p < 0.001), and the static TWFE regression gives 0.185. Without Malaysia, the MTB effect is −0.003 (p = 0.940) and the market capitalization effect 0.037.
In the outcome regression, a country or industry indicator would be unidentified in a cell with no control firm in that country or industry, and its coefficient would be set to zero. This does not occur: in all 34 cells of event years 0 to 3 for ln(MTB), and all 34 for ln(market capitalization), every indicator carried by a scored firm is also carried by a control firm.
