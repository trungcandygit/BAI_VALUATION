## Three most likely new referee objections (job 5; S1-99 to S1-100)

1. **Which dating is economically right.** By the paper's own argument g + 1 is the earliest year a score could be public, so the pre-registered g dating puts a year that certainly precedes publication in the post window. The g + 1 decline (−6.6%) is "an accounting identity" equal to minus the year-g rise, which is itself pre-publication. Expect: "make g + 1 primary for the investor-observable question, or explain why the year-g rise is not part of the run-up". See R6S1-16.
2. **Survivorship.** Every firm in the extract has data in 2024, and delisted firms are missing. Compustat Global carries inactive firms. A referee will ask for a re-extraction that includes delisted firms, or a bound on the bias, because a control group of survivors can manufacture the scored-firm run-up or hide its reversal. "Cannot sign the bias" will not satisfy them.
3. **Leaning on a post hoc wave contrast.** The wave split was drawn after seeing the counts. The Malaysian run-up interval (−7.4% to 15.2%) contains the pooled 10%. The "other cohorts" are not distinguishable from Malaysia in run-up (−0.075, CI −0.219 to 0.075), yet they show the g + 1 decline (−0.111). The claim that "the decline appears only in the waves that had a run-up" and the paper's "most weight" on this contrast (3.3) will be challenged as a multiple-testing and forking-paths problem. See R6S1-17.
