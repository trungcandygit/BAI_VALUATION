## Simulated referees: three most likely new objections (S1-99 to S1-103)

1. **Identification (bad-control timing of size).** The run-up, now the paper's central finding, is estimated conditional on log total assets in the base year g−1. That is after the run-up, and the paper says entry follows size growth. The supplement shows the run-up falls to 6.6% without covariates (event −5 p = 0.068) and to 8.0% with size at g−3 (pre-trend p = 0.176) (supplement:255). Conditioning on a size level reached during the run-up can manufacture or inflate a relative MTB run-up. The referee would ask for the run-up conditioned on size at g−5 or g−6 (before the window), or matched on pre-window levels, and for the selection claim to be stated for that specification. Evidence that would resolve it: a pre-window-covariate run-up row in Table 5 or S4.
2. **Field (what drives LSEG entry; survivorship).** Without LSEG's own coverage-expansion history (e.g. the dates of universe extensions in 2019-2022) and point-in-time data, "selection on growth" cannot be told apart from provider-wide universe expansions that pick up firms by index membership or size cut-off at a given date. Survivorship to 2024 (manuscript:31) also drops delisted never-scored firms, which are probably the weakest performers. That removal would make never-scored growth look better and so shrink the run-up, but the paper says it cannot sign the bias. Expect requests for (a) LSEG methodology or coverage-history documentation, (b) a robustness check that limits controls to firms listed throughout 2014-2024, or a bound for attrition.
3. **Generalist (exploratory evidence carrying the argument; contribution size).** The wave split was drawn after seeing the data (manuscript:72), yet 3.3 and the Discussion use the wave contrast "as evidence" (manuscript:40; letter Comment 7). Many exploratory splits are reported without multiplicity control, including the one with a significant gain (#17). A referee will ask for a pre-specified or multiplicity-adjusted version of the wave contrast and may question whether "selection exists and the effect is unidentified" is enough for publication without a quantified bias benchmark for naive scored-vs-unscored designs. The TWFE 6.6% is the natural candidate and could be made a headline deliverable.

Simulated verdict (unsoftened): major revision is likely to become minor if #1-#5 are fixed. Objection 1 could reopen the main claim if the run-up disappears with pre-window covariates.
