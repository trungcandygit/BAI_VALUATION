@@ abstract
Purpose|Environmental, social, and governance (ESG) scores decide which firms ESG-screened investors can hold, yet most listed emerging-market firms have none. This study asks which firms enter the scored universe and whether entry changes their market valuation.

Design/methodology/approach|The study compares {{n_treated_all}} non-financial firms in Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, and Thailand that received a first London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG) ESG score in 2015 to 2024 with {{n_never}} never-scored firms, before and after the first score, following a pre-analysis plan.

Findings|From five years before the first score to the year before it, scored firms' market-to-book (MTB) ratio grew {{mtb_runup_m5}} percent more than that of never-scored firms (95% interval {{mtb_runup_lo}} to {{mtb_runup_hi}} percent); a large Malaysian wave shows no such run-up. Under parallel trends, MTB did not rise after the first score (estimate {{mtb_att}}; interval {{mtb_lo}} to {{mtb_hi}}), and we detect no difference between high- and low-score firms (p {{mtb_diff_p_txt}}). Dated one year later, MTB fell {{mtb_R10_pct}} percent, a decline that small departures from parallel trends erase.

Research limitations/implications|The provider chooses whom to cover, the data are annual, and departures from parallel trends like those before coverage admit effects of several percent.

Practical implications|Rating providers, ESG screens, index builders, and ESG-premium studies that use this provider's scores draw on firms that have often just outgrown their peers.

Originality/value|The study documents selection into ESG rating coverage, and its valuation consequences, in emerging markets.
